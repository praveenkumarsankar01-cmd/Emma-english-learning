Part 1

How to use this guide

Most students don't need more grammar. They need more practice saying things out loud, and a way to notice their own mistakes. This guide gives you both. Use it before class, after class, and on the days in between.

The 3-step speaking routine

Do this for 15 minutes a day. It works better than one long session a week.

1

Say it

Pick one question from the list below and answer it out loud for one minute. Don't stop to fix mistakes. Keep going.

2

Record it

Answer the same question again and record yourself on your phone. Listen back once.

3

Fix one thing

Choose one thing to improve: a word, a sound or a sentence. Say the answer a third time with that one fix.

Questions to answer

What did you do last weekend?What's your job, and what do you like about it?
Describe the place where you live.What's something you learned recently?
Tell me about a good meal you had.What would you change about your city?
Tip from Emma: Speak first, check later. If you stop to translate every sentence, you practise translating, not speaking.

Part 2

30 phrases for small talk

Learn these as whole phrases, not word by word. Say each one out loud three times.

Starting

  1. How's your week going?
  2. Have you been busy today?
  3. Did you do anything nice at the weekend?
  4. How do you know Sam?
  5. Is this your first time here?
  6. That looks good. What is it?
  7. How was the traffic this morning?
  8. Have you worked here long?
  9. I love your bag. Where's it from?
  10. Lovely weather today, isn't it?

Keeping it going

  1. Oh really? How come?
  2. That sounds fun. What was the best part?
  3. I know what you mean.
  4. Me too. / Me neither.
  5. What made you choose that?
  6. How did that go?
  7. That's interesting. Tell me more.
  8. I've never tried that. Is it hard?
  9. Sorry, could you say that again?
  10. So what's next for you?

Leaving politely

  1. It was really nice talking to you.
  2. I'd better get going.
  3. I'll let you get back to work.
  4. Let's catch up properly soon.
  5. Good luck with the interview.
  6. Enjoy the rest of your day.
  7. See you on Monday.
  8. Say hi to your family for me.
  9. Let me know how it goes.
  10. Take care. Bye for now.
Practice: Pick one phrase from each column. Use all three in a real conversation this week, at work, in a shop or on a call.

Part 3

Sounds that matter

You don't need a perfect accent. You need to be easy to understand. These sound pairs cause the most confusion.

Sound pairSay the differenceTry this sentence
ship / sheepshort "i" / long "ee"The sheep are on the ship.
live / leaveshort "i" / long "ee"I live here, but I leave at six.
full / foolshort "u" / long "oo"Don't be a fool, the cup is full.
bed / bad"e" / open "a"A bad night in a hard bed.
think / sinktongue between teeth for "th"I think the sink is broken.
very / berrytop teeth on lip for "v"A very big berry.
light / righttongue up for "l", back for "r"Turn right at the light.
walk / work"aw" / "er"I walk to work.

Word stress

Put the stress on the right part of the word and people understand you, even if a sound is not perfect.

photographphotographyphotographic
present (a gift)present (to show)presentation
comfortablevegetableinteresting
Tip from Emma: Record the sentences above, then listen with your eyes closed. Which words are hard to hear? Practise only those.
